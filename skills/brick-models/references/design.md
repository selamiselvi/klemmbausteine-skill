# Designing readable assemblies

Work from a recognizable silhouette, then structural masses, then selected details. A small model with intentional proportions is preferable to arbitrary decoration. Use a controlled palette and avoid hidden filler that does not improve stiffness.

## Select parts for fidelity at the chosen scale

Translate defining reference features into approximate dimensions at the agreed model size, then compare suitable real parts and assemblies. Search a parts catalog for relevant shapes and techniques, including slopes, curves, arches, tiles, brackets or sideways construction where useful; do not restrict the search to familiar rectangular pieces. Verify candidate part IDs, dimensions, geometry, connection orientation and whether the required part/color combination exists. Use catalog records and geometry sources such as Rebrickable and LDraw as evidence, not memory or a plausible part name alone. A catalog record does not prove current stock or a physically sound connection.

Keep concise feature-to-part choices and unresolved limitations with the private working brief. For features that dominate likeness, prototype competing constructions at the same scale and compare their silhouette, openings, proportions and buildability against the reference. Choose parts for the improvement they provide, not for novelty or to inflate the piece count. Retain requested size and explicit collection, budget or complexity limits. Recommend a larger size only when a specific feature needs more space after suitable parts and construction options have been considered; merely scaling up the same geometry does not improve detail.

Distinguish an unavailable real part from an unsupported engine capability. Do not add a catalog ID and render it as a generic block, or assume the current rectangular collision/support checks validate a new shape or connection. Identify and test the required tool support before using it in a final assembly. State unresolved limitations without calling the result the most detailed model possible.

## Connections and instructions

For regular bricks, alternate seam positions across layers. Long crossing foundation plates tie smaller units together; plates merely touching side-to-side are not connected. Overhangs need enough studs below them, and thin towers need a broad, interlocked base. The validator reports partial support but cannot judge tipping or clutch strength.

Order construction from accessible lower structures upward. Each step should introduce a spatially coherent change. Separate symmetric but distant additions when one camera cannot show them clearly. Use the optional step view for the far side. Before a roof hides an interior, show that interior's completed step.

Group repeated part/color combinations in the step inventory. In technical instructions, keep previously assembled pieces in their actual colors and use the new-part edge highlights for orientation. Inspect white-on-white boundaries and dark plates in particular. Keep the camera stable and signal necessary changes. When a placement is hidden, change the view or split the step; a colored outline cannot make hidden geometry visible.

The legacy studio style also displays numbered placement maps. Technical guides retain those maps as machine-readable companion files but keep coordinates out of the normal reading flow. Add a short note only when the image cannot communicate a connection or rotation.

Judge the result from all four sides. A pleasing front render cannot compensate for a disconnected rear structure, invisible instruction placement or a mismatched bill of materials. Start with a small test; expand the part palette only with dimensions, catalog references, validation and export tests for the new geometry.
